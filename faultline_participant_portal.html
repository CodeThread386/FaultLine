How FaultLine Works

Two phases. One brutal truth — you cannot build good software until you truly understand how bad software destroys you.

Pick a track. Build the most broken, unmaintainable, and deliberately terrible software you can — while keeping it functional. Bad architecture, cursed UX, spaghetti logic. Every bad decision is the goal.

FAQ

Questions

What does "functional" mean for Phase 1?↓
Your system must launch, complete its core user flow, and not crash on basic input. Everything else — how broken, confusing, or infuriating it is — is entirely up to you.
What stack can we use?↓
Frontend: HTML/CSS/JS, React, or Next.js. Backend: Python, Java, or JavaScript. Any database or none at all. Full freedom within these options.
How does the codebase swap work?↓
During lunch, organizers assign each team a codebase from another team in the same track. You get their GitHub repo link and a one-paragraph description of what the system is supposed to do. That's it. No other hints.
What if our track has no other team to swap with?↓
Organizers have pre-built cursed codebases for every track. You'll receive one of these if there's no same-track team to swap with. They're built to match the difficulty of what real teams produce.
Do we have to do the main stage games?↓
No. Games are completely opt-in. Team members who want to participate can step away. Everyone else can keep building. Both groups are welcome.
If we don't make Phase 1 finals, are we out?↓
No. Phase 1 and Phase 2 are completely independent. Every team that starts Phase 1 competes in Phase 2. You can win a Phase 2 award even if you never made Phase 1 finals.
